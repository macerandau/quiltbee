App niche validation

Quilt Designer Validation

Run 23 September 2026. Evidence: App Store search API, 514 App Store reviews across 20 apps, Google autocomplete, the Craft Industry Alliance 2024 survey, and vendor pricing pages.

Verdict: conditional go. The product gap is confirmed and the price ceiling is clear. Demand for "quilt design app" is real, the best-selling incumbent has not shipped an update in ten years and crashes on save, and quilters spell out exactly what they want in their reviews. The risk is not the product. It is distribution: 26 quilt apps launched in the past year and 18 of them have fewer than 10 ratings. The winner will be decided in Facebook groups, guilds and YouTube, not in App Store search.

The spec writes itself from the unhappy reviews:

  • Zero-tutorial first block. Drag, drop, undo. Reviewers with computer science degrees gave up on Quiltler; several say they went back to graph paper and colored pencils.
  • Sashing width, border width, on-point setting. Requested for eight years across every app and still missing from the incumbent.
  • Real shapes in the block editor. Rectangles for log cabin, hexagons, flying geese, curves. Not clip-art animals.
  • Fabric photos with a scale reference, so a print renders at true size and can be repositioned inside a patch.
  • Yardage, not piece counts. Quiltography's yardage is the one thing people praise; Quiltler's piece count is the thing they curse.
  • PDF export that works, and cloud backup so a new iPad does not erase a stash that took weeks to photograph.
  • One-time price. This audience rates apps down for subscriptions and rates Quiltography up "for the price." A $15 to $20 purchase with an optional cloud tier is the safe shape.

6. Pricing and distribution

Price

$19.99 one-time for the full editor and yardage. Sits above Quiltography's $14.99, which reviewers call a bargain, and far under EQ8's $239.95. An optional $14.99 per year cloud tier covers backup across devices and manufacturer fabric libraries, which are the two things that cost you money to run. Never gate a feature after purchase; that single move earned Quiltler 2 its worst reviews.

Distribution

App Store search is where the 18 dead apps live. The audience lives in Facebook quilting groups, guild newsletters, YouTube tutorial channels and quilt shops. Forty of the 127 quilt apps are shop storefronts, which means shops already believe in apps and will co-promote a tool that sells more fabric. Missouri Star's free app has 34,000 ratings; that audience is the target, and a tutorial channel is the way in.

7. Risks and gaps in the evidence

  • TideStitch and StitchLogic launched this year with the same read. Neither has traction yet, but one could get a guild endorsement first. Speed and usability, not features, decide this.
  • Reddit was unreachable from this session, so community discussion is inferred from reviews only. Worth ten minutes of reading r/quilting by hand before committing.
  • Android demand exists ("quilt design app free for android" autocompletes) but the paying iPad audience comes first.
  • Fabric photos at true scale is a small computer-vision feature and the one piece that is not plain arithmetic. Ship with a manual ruler calibration and improve later.

8. Next step

A working block editor and yardage calculator as a web prototype, one week of effort, put in front of five quilters. If three of them build a block without asking a question, build the app. If they reach for graph paper, stop.
